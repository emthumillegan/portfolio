Strategy / Experience

Spectacles.

A new way to see the holidays.

Pitched to Snapchat Spectacles AR glasses a way to reach general audiences through holiday events

This pitch imagined how Snapchat Spectacles could reach broader audiences by tying AR experiences to holiday events. The concept leaned into seasonal family spectacles, like christmas light shows and halloween haunted houses.

The work connected immersive technology to familiar cultural moments. I pushed for a narrative that would make AR feel more approachable, especially since it's enhancing trusted experiences rather than replacing them.

Contribution
Work on concept development, the pitch, the video, and the research.

An invitation to experience the holidays differently

The opening sets a seasonal, immersive tone for a concept pitch about Spectacles. Familiar holiday environments become the entry point for introducing augmented reality.

SECTION 02 / 21

Bringing the product into the story

The title treatment connects Spectacles to the holiday theme. The goal is to make the technology feel part of an experience people already want to share.

SECTION 03 / 21

Starting with a moment of imagination

This transition asks the audience to picture an experience before hearing the strategy. It creates a pause between the pitch introduction and the proposed activations.

SECTION 04 / 21

The device as a window into the experience

The product visual introduces the hardware at the center of the concept. It grounds the upcoming installations in a physical object visitors could use.

SECTION 05 / 21

A world of seasonal possibilities

The moodboard gathers lanterns, light displays, and immersive seasonal spaces. These references establish the kinds of settings where the pitch imagines shared AR experiences.

SECTION 06 / 21

Learning from another wearable camera

The GoPro case study provides a reference for introducing a new way to capture experiences. The comparison is used as a strategic starting point for the pitch.

SECTION 07 / 21

Letting participants carry the story

The pitch examines the idea of customers becoming a distribution channel through their own content. For Spectacles, the opportunity is to make participation itself worth sharing.

SECTION 08 / 21

Recognizable moments, recognizable value

The collage shows the visual language of action and first-person experience. It helps identify what makes wearable-camera content immediately understandable.

SECTION 09 / 21

Where that model leaves room to grow

The critique identifies limitations in solo, recorded experiences and a narrow activity niche. Those observations frame the pitch’s proposed shift toward live, shared participation.

SECTION 10 / 21

From recording a moment to sharing one

The strategy adapts first-person capture into a more social proposition. Everyday and seasonal environments broaden the range of moments the concept can address.

SECTION 11 / 21

Spectacles as a shared experience

The central message emphasizes people experiencing something together. This is the bridge between the strategic analysis and the proposed holiday installations.

SECTION 12 / 21

Showing how the idea travels

The social mockups translate the installations into a familiar discovery format. They show how a visitor might encounter the event before deciding to attend.

SECTION 13 / 21

Sequencing the rollout

The timeline section moves from the idea itself to how it could be introduced. Each activation is paired with a proposed period for promotion and participation.

SECTION 14 / 21

Activation one: Haunted Trails

The first proposed installation uses a Halloween setting to introduce the experience. An existing seasonal attraction provides an accessible reason to try the technology.

SECTION 15 / 21

Planning the Halloween launch

This schedule sequences social promotion, creator invitations, opening, and closing. It shows the proposed relationship between awareness and the live experience.

SECTION 16 / 21

Activation two: Phoenix Zoo

The second proposal shifts into a winter light-display setting. The visual direction keeps the shared-experience message while changing the seasonal context.

SECTION 17 / 21

Planning the winter launch

The winter schedule repeats the promotional structure around a different event window. A consistent rollout pattern would make the series easier to communicate.

SECTION 18 / 21

Activation three: The Arboretum

The third installation uses a lantern-filled environment as another entry point into AR. The pitch explores how the concept can travel across distinct visual settings.

SECTION 19 / 21

Planning the lantern-festival launch

The final activation timeline connects advance promotion with a defined period of public access. These dates represent the proposed campaign schedule, not completed events.

SECTION 20 / 21

Reaching beyond early adopters

The adoption curve frames the challenge of moving a new technology toward a wider audience. Familiar seasonal experiences are the pitch’s proposed bridge.

SECTION 21 / 21

Closing on the shared moment

The final slide brings the installations back into one campaign story. The concept aims to make Spectacles understandable through an experience visitors can participate in together.
